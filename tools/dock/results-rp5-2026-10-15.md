## ARP conflict as the cause of the 10-08 "router goes silent to one client" (HYPOTHESIS, untested)
Cliff (~01:35): "how about our networking issues when unplugging and replugging the dock? Are they possibly some
similar type of issue?" Signature on 10-08 01:22 boot (HANDOFF 2026-10-08 02:20): NM full at 14 s, limited at
334 s; the device's ARP to the gateway answered, fusor/BONESAW/rizz/drip pingable, only the gateway silent to
ICMP/TCP/UDP from this client; `nmcli connection up ozma` fixed it in 3 s. That is what a stale ARP entry AT THE
ROUTER pointing at the dock/hub NIC's MAC produces after an undock: the device still resolves the router, Linux
hosts re-resolve .68 within seconds (unicast/broadcast probes), the router keeps sending .68's traffic to the
gone MAC, and a profile re-activation sends gratuitous ARP which updates it. The chain that makes the LAN learn
the wrong MAC is the 10-14 root cause (reference-rp5-wlan-route-arp-conflict): the dock NIC answers ARP for
.68 (arp_ignore=0), NM's conflict detection pulls the wlan0 subnet route, and from then on .68-sourced traffic
leaves through the dock port with the dock MAC. The hub ethernet (.47) was in use that week and the same handoff
has docked Dark Souls runs. If true: the wifi heal ladder (bsp-common 26-33, which caused the 10-05 dead wifi
after wake by PCIe-resetting the chip) treated this aftermath, and bsp-common 35 (arp_ignore=1/arp_announce=2)
removes the cause. Unknowns: whether the hub was attached at that 01:22 boot (record silent); why the router
stayed stale ~20 min (a Linux neighbor table would not; flow offload or a per-client IP binding would).
